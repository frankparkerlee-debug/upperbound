# Strategy — Standalone Peptide Telehealth Venture

*Working name: **Upperbound**. Drafted 2026-07-31. Separate company from Merit, Halo, and Conduit.*

---

## 1. The one-sentence thesis

Every credible player in this category sells a **deficiency** story to a 45-year-old man. Nobody sells a **capacity** story to a 32-year-old professional of either gender — and that is the buyer with the most money, the most years of subscription ahead of them, and the least brand loyalty.

---

## 2. Why now, precisely now

The FDA's Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee met **July 23–24, 2026 — last week** — and voted to recommend six peptides for the 503A bulk drug substances list:

| Peptide | Vote | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| BPC-157 | 8-6-1 | Recommended |
| KPV | 8-6-1 | Recommended |
| TB-500 | 8-6-1 | Recommended |
| MOTS-c | 7-5-2 | Recommended |
| Epitalon | 7-4-1 | Recommended |
| Semax | 8-5-1 | Recommended |
| Emideltide (DSIP) | 6-7-1 | **Not** recommended |

FDA's own scientists opposed all additions, but the agency rarely diverges from PCAC recommendations. Formal notice-and-comment rulemaking follows and may not conclude until **2027 or later**; enforcement discretion may be announced in the interim. A second PCAC lands **before February 2027** for injectable GHK-Cu, LL-37, dihexa, PEG-MGF, and melanotan II.

**What this means for us, stated plainly:**

- Those six are **recommended, not yet legal**. Anyone selling BPC-157 today is ahead of the law. We will not be.
- We launch on what is compoundable **right now** and are positioned — brand built, pharmacy contracted, protocols drafted, patients enrolled — on the day the rule lands.
- Needham sizes the addressable peptide market at ~$30B. Hims & Hers has a peptide facility operating since Feb 2025 and its CMO testified at the July PCAC. **Assume a mass-market entrant lands within 12–18 months of the final rule.** The brand moat has to exist before the category commoditizes.

That is the window. Roughly 12 months to build a brand that a well-funded incumbent cannot simply outspend.

### What we launch on (legal today, 503A Category 1)

Sermorelin · Ipamorelin · Thymosin alpha-1 · PT-141 (bremelanotide) · Injectable NAD+ · Selank · Kisspeptin-10 · Injectable glutathione · Enclomiphene · LDN

### What we stage for the rule

BPC-157 · TB-500 · KPV · MOTS-c · Epitalon · Semax

### What we do not touch

**Tesamorelin.** It is FDA-approved as Egrifta, so compounding it is not a bulks-list question — it is an "essentially a copy of a commercially available drug" question, permitted only for a documented patient-specific need. Do not build a product line on it.

**Mass-market compounded GLP-1s.** Shortages were declared resolved (tirzepatide late 2024, semaglutide Feb 2025), removing the legal basis. Novo and Lilly are litigating; state boards are investigating. If GLP-1s appear here at all it is 503A patient-specific with documented clinical need — never as the growth engine.

## 3. The competitive map, and the empty cell

| | Male-coded | Genuinely unisex |
|---|---|---|
| **Clinical authority** | Marek Health, Defy, Maximus, Blokes | *(empty)* |
| **Consumer polish** | PeterMD | Function Health, Superpower — **but neither prescribes** |
| **Commodity/price** | Strut | Eden, Ivim |

Three findings drive everything downstream:

**Finding 1 — the authority/design gap.** Marek Health has the most clinical credibility in the category and a dark, data-dense interface that looks like a lab report. Function Health has the best design in adjacent health and does not prescribe anything. Nobody has both. That is the cell we take.

**Finding 2 — "unisex" is fake everywhere.** Today it is executed as two doors: Hone runs Men's/Women's navigation; Joi and Blokes are literally two separate brands. Eden and Ivim are unisex only by being generic. **Nobody sells one protocol system, one vocabulary, one brand to a 33-year-old woman and a 33-year-old man.** Function and Superpower prove that reads well to this demographic — they just stop at diagnostics.

**Finding 3 — everyone is peptide-*adjacent*, not peptide-*native*.** Every incumbent's clinical spine is hormone therapy (TRT/BHRT) or GLP-1, with peptides bolted on as an upsell. Ivim's peptide line is literally a waitlist. A protocol architecture designed peptide-first is structurally differentiated, not just differently marketed.

## 4. Audience

**The under-recovered professional.** 28–38. Genuinely 50/50 men and women. Income $150k+. Consulting, law, medicine, finance, tech, founders.

They are not aging and they know it. They are **running a sustained load their recovery cannot match** — travel, sleep debt, cortisol, training they no longer adapt to, cognitive output that used to be free. They read the research. They have a Whoop or an Oura and are bored of being told their recovery is 41%. They have money and no patience.

**The insight that unlocks the copy:** a 32-year-old does not think of themselves as declining. They think of themselves as *behind on recovery*. Every incumbent sells the 45-year-old's story — "your testosterone is low, we can restore it." That story does not land on this buyer, and every brand in the category is telling it.

**What they will not tolerate:** archetype names (Maximus's King/Warrior/Lover), gym-bro coding, guru energy, discount-code retail energy, being sold a deficiency they do not feel, and — critically — **12-month prepaid plans they cannot cancel.**

## 5. Positioning

> **A capacity brand, not a decline brand.**
> Physician-guided pathways to greater capacity, for people whose output is their livelihood.

*Revised 2026-07-31 (second revision): **pathways posture, for LegitScript.** The marketing surface sells the pathway — a physician-guided care plan with phases and checkpoints — never the medication. No compound names anywhere on public marketing pages (they now live only in clinical/formulary contexts behind intake). Treatment is "prescribed if appropriate, included in the pathway price." This is both the LegitScript-safe posture and, usefully, a cleaner brand: competitors sell vials, we sell the climb. The peptide-native architecture remains the operational truth; it is simply no longer the marketing language.*

**The frame:** you have a ceiling. It is not fixed. We design a deliberate, sequenced protocol to move it, and a physician decides at each checkpoint whether it continues.

*Revised 2026-07-31. The earlier frame — "we measure where it is and show you the number moving" — is no longer available to us and must not appear anywhere in copy. With no diagnostic testing, any claim about a marker moving is unsupportable and, given the FDA's 2026 warning-letter activity against telehealth marketing, actively dangerous. The honest version of the frame is about **deliberate protocol design**, not measured outcomes.*

**Category vocabulary to avoid entirely** (measured saturation across the competitive set):

| Term | Status |
|---|---|
| optimization / optimize | Fatigued — Marek, Maximus, Hone, Ivim, Defy. Reads male-gym by 2026. |
| longevity / healthspan | Fatigued **and age-coding** — actively repels the 28–38 buyer. |
| "100+ biomarkers" | Commodity. Function 100+, Superpower 100+, Marek 65–100+, Joi 110. Number inflation is a dead race. |
| personalized / individualized / tailored to you | Dead. Eden, Ivim, Strut, and Joi all use it verbatim. |
| physician-led, US-licensed, compounded in the USA | Table stakes, not differentiation. |
| root cause / proactive not reactive | Functional-medicine cliché. |

**Vocabulary that is genuinely unclaimed:** capacity · load · recovery · ceiling · reserve · throughput · adaptation · output · resilience. Nobody in the category uses performance-science language without also using bro-optimization language. That combination is available.

## 6. The four wedges

**Wedge 1 — Protocol design, not product selection.** *(Revised 2026-07-31: no diagnostic testing. Labs-as-gate is off the table by decision.)* The differentiator is the **sequence** — which compounds, at what doses, in what order, for how long, and when to stop — rather than a vial chosen off a menu. Every async competitor sells SKUs; a cycled, checkpointed 12-week protocol is a genuinely different object.

**This is a materially weaker wedge than labs and should be understood as such.** Without measurement we forfeit the strongest legitimacy signal in the category, the premium justification, and the data asset. Wedges 2–4 and brand quality now carry the whole load, and none of them are defensible against a well-funded entrant for long. Revisit at-home collection or wearable-derived tracking as soon as operations allow — it is the single highest-value thing that can be added back.

**Wedge 2 — Monthly. Cancel anytime. At a premium price.** The category discounts through prepayment — Ivim's 12-month prepay is ~44% off month-to-month, Maximus annual is 50% off. This generates the category's single loudest complaint: no mid-term cancellation. Charging more for the freedom to leave is a confidence signal that costs us nothing but nerve, and it is unattackable by anyone whose unit economics depend on prepaid lock-in.

**Wedge 3 — Peptide-native protocol architecture.** Not "TRT with peptides available." Protocols composed peptide-first, sequenced and cycled, with the hormone layer as one input among several.

**Wedge 4 — The number moves.** Every member has a small set of tracked markers and sees them move. Not 100 biomarkers — that race is lost and boring. Six to ten that map directly to the protocol, retested quarterly, charted. Function sells the panel; we sell the *delta*.

## 7. Business model

Anchor against observed category norms: membership fees converge at **$75–155/mo**, all-in competitive number is **$150–300/mo**.

| Component | Price | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Intake | **$0** | No panel to justify a fee. PeterMD's free-consult model is the async competitive reality. |
| Protocol, all-in | **$189–249/mo** | One number: physician, protocol design, medication, shipping, checkpoints. No membership stacked on top. |
| **All-in** | **$189–249/mo** | Upper-middle of the observed $150–300 band. Premium without a lab story to defend a higher number. |

*Revised 2026-07-31. The original $249 intake assumed a baseline panel; with no testing it is unjustifiable friction. Folding membership into a single protocol price also removes the "what am I paying $99 for?" objection that async brands without labs consistently face.*

**Never discount through lock-in.** No 12-month prepay. If we discount, it is for lab compliance or referral — behaviors we want — never for surrendering the right to cancel.

**Unit economics to validate before scaling:** per-consult cost from MD Integrations or Beluga, per-panel cost through Junction, and pharmacy COGS. The $249 intake should approximately cover first-consult plus panel, making CAC the only real acquisition cost.

## 8. Compliance posture — non-negotiable

The FDA is now enforcing on **marketing**, not just product: 30 warning letters on March 3, 2026 and 25 more on June 16, 2026 to telehealth firms whose promotion implied equivalence to approved drugs or obscured the actual compounder.

1. **Do not private-label the vial.** Ivim took a warning letter on Feb 20, 2026 for putting its own brand name on compounded product labels — misbranding. The pharmacy's name goes on the vial. Ours does not. *(Note: this is the opposite of Merit's practice and a hard rule here.)*
2. **Name the pharmacy partner** publicly. Named 503A/503B partner, accreditations shown.
3. **LegitScript certification**, plus PCAB/ACHC pharmacy accreditation, displayed in the footer. Strut Health is the template for the badge stack. This gates Google Ads and is read by Google, Meta, Visa, and Mastercard.
4. **Explicit "compounded ≠ FDA-approved" disclosure** wherever a compound is named.
5. **Labs before prescribing**, documented.
6. Never imply equivalence to an approved drug. Attribute any trial outcome to the *drug studied in the trial*, never to our preparation.

### Channel reality

- **Google is the channel.** It materially loosened in 2026: the March 31, 2026 recurring-billing update permits certified US pharmacies to run subscription and consultation-included offers, and the April 2026 update expanded telemedicine geographies. LegitScript is the key to the door.
- **Meta is structurally hostile** and got worse — in December 2025, 35 state attorneys general demanded stricter enforcement against compounded GLP-1 advertising. Do not plan named-molecule creative there.
- **Naming molecules on our own site is fine.** Eden, Strut, Joi, and AgelessRx all name compounds openly. Legitimate prescribers do this because their ad exposure is Google-with-LegitScript rather than Meta catalog. *(This is a real divergence from Merit's constraint, which exists because of Meta. Do not carry Merit's rule over reflexively.)*

## 9. Separation from the other brands

Merit, Halo, Conduit, and this venture stay entirely distinct — separate entities, repos, hosting accounts, databases, domains, ad accounts, payment processors, and sending domains. No shared pixel. No shared brand assets or copy.

The closest adjacency to manage is **Conduit**, which serves an overlapping demographic. The separation that matters:

| | Conduit | Upperbound |
|---|---|---|
| Frame | Regenerative health, becoming | Capacity under load |
| Entry point | Weight loss / GLP-1 (~80%) | Recovery + cognitive/physical output |
| Delivery | Pen, single peptide, no reconstitution | Protocol, lab-gated, multi-input |
| Labs | Supporting | **The gate** |
| Register | Warm institution, professionally whimsical | Precise, peer-technical, instrument-like |
| Molecules named | Never (Meta constraint) | Openly (Google/LegitScript posture) |

If a decision would make the two brands look related, it is the wrong decision.

## 10. Open decisions for Parker and partners

1. **The name.** Shortlist and verified domain availability in `BRAND.md` §1. Everything else can proceed in parallel; this gates domain purchase and entity formation.
2. **Payments.** Merit is PayPal-only for hard reasons that do not obviously travel to a legitimate physician-prescribed telehealth entity. Stripe is generally available to this category and makes subscription billing far easier. Confirm the constraint stops at Merit.
3. **Own medical group vs. vendor network.** Phase 1 uses MD Integrations or Beluga physicians. Your own PC/MSO gives better margin and control at real legal cost. Decide when, not whether.
4. **Which compounding pharmacy.** Sign this *first* — before the telehealth vendor. Capacity is not the constraint (Empower, Strive, ReviveRx, Hallandale, and Revelation are all pre-positioning for the rule); accreditation, formulary, and willingness to take our volume are.
5. **LegitScript timing.** It needs a named physician entity, a named pharmacy, and a street address — the same three blockers currently sitting on Conduit's application. Start it the week the entity exists; it gates paid acquisition entirely.
