# Brand Book v0 — Upperbound

*Drafted 2026-07-31. Working name pending partner sign-off. Deliberately built to share no DNA with Merit, Halo, or Conduit.*

---

## 1. Name

### Recommendation: **Upperbound**

An upper bound is the mathematical term for the ceiling of a set — the limit a value cannot exceed. The brand's entire proposition is that in physiology, unlike in mathematics, **that bound is a variable.**

Why it wins:

- **It states the strategy.** Capacity, not decline. The category sells you a deficit; we sell you a ceiling that moves.
- **It is coded to the buyer.** Quant, precise, slightly technical — native to how consultants, engineers, lawyers, and finance people think. It flatters intelligence instead of masculinity.
- **It is genuinely unisex.** No archetype, no gender, no age. A 33-year-old woman and a 33-year-old man both read it the same way.
- **It collides with nothing.** Zero overlap with the fatigued category vocabulary — no optimize, no longevity, no biomarker count.
- **It gives us a mark.** The literal notation for a bound is a horizontal line above a value. The logo is a rule above the wordmark, and the brand device is that **the line moves up**. That is a real, ownable, animatable identity — not a lockup with a leaf in it.

**Verified available (RDAP, 2026-07-31):** `upperbound.health` ✅ · `upperboundhealth.com` ✅

### Alternates, verified

| Name | Domains | Read |
|---|---|---|
| **Ceiling** | `ceiling.health` ✅ · `ceilinghealth.com` ✅ · `joinceiling.com` ✅ | Same idea, shorter and warmer. Weakness: the bare noun reads as a *cap*, and the mark is mundane. Strongest fallback — the full domain set is free, which is rare. |
| **Auvra** | `auvrahealth.com` ✅ | Coined, premium, unisex, meaningless. Meaningless is a cost: you pay in advertising to install the meaning. Only pick this if the partners want zero semantic baggage. |
| **Deload** | `deloadhealth.com` ✅ | Training term for a planned recovery week — perfectly on-theme and insider-credible. Weakness: it means *do less*, which is a strange promise, and it is gym-literate in a way that narrows the audience. |

### What the domain search actually found

Every clean one-word `.com` in health is registered — Caliber, Threshold, Aperture, Praxis, Axiom, Verve, Cadre, Arete, Reserve, Capacity, Compound, Yield, and roughly forty others all came back taken, most of them parked rather than operating. Even coined bare `.com`s are squatted.

Two consequences worth budgeting for:
1. **A `<name>health.com` or `.health` construction is the realistic path** at registration price. This is normal in 2026 and the category does it (`tryeden.com`, `honehealth.com`, `joiandblokes.com`).
2. **Many of the best names are purchasable, not unavailable.** A parked premium domain typically runs $2k–25k. If the partners want a bare one-word `.com`, that is a budget line, not a blocker — say so and I will run acquisition inquiries on a shortlist.

## 2. Essence

### Why Upperbound exists

Because the people with the most demanding lives are being sold a story about getting old, when their actual problem is that their recovery no longer matches their load.

### The proposition

**Peptide-native protocols, physician-prescribed and lab-gated, for people whose output is their livelihood.**

Three commitments underneath it:

1. **We measure first.** No prescription without bloodwork. Ever.
2. **We show the delta.** Not a wall of 100 markers — the six to ten that map to your protocol, retested quarterly, charted over time.
3. **We never lock you in.** Monthly. Cancel anytime. At a premium price.

### What Upperbound is not

- Not a longevity brand *(that is a 45-year-old's story and an age-coded word)*
- Not a men's hormone clinic with peptides bolted on
- Not an async questionnaire that ships a vial in 48 hours
- Not a diagnostics membership that hands you a PDF and no physician
- Not a discount funnel with a 12-month prepay trap

## 3. Voice

### Line

> **Your ceiling is a variable.**

### Hero

> **You're not aging. You're under-recovered.**
> Peptide protocols, prescribed against your bloodwork — for the load you're actually carrying.

### Register

**Peer-technical.** Upperbound speaks the way a very good specialist speaks to a very smart patient: precise, unhurried, assuming intelligence, never performing authority.

- Not warm-institutional (that is Conduit's register — do not borrow it)
- Not coach-voice ("let's go get it")
- Not guru-voice
- Not embarrassment-soft ("we know this is awkward")
- Not lab-cold either — precision is a form of respect, not a temperature

### Tone

- **Precise before persuasive.** A real number beats an adjective every time.
- **Declarative.** Statements, not questions. No "ready to feel your best?"
- **Unhurried.** No urgency theater, no countdowns, no scarcity.
- **Specific about limits.** We say what a protocol will not do. That is the most persuasive thing on the page.
- **Technical without jargon-flexing.** Use the real word, then earn it in the next clause.

### Sample copy in register

**Headlines:**
- "Your ceiling is a variable."
- "Measure it. Then move it."
- "Recovery is a capacity, not a mood."
- "The protocol follows the panel."
- "Six markers. Every quarter. Charted."

**Subheads:**
- "We draw blood before we prescribe anything. It costs us conversions and it is not negotiable."
- "Sermorelin, ipamorelin, thymosin alpha-1 — prescribed by a physician who read your labs, dispensed by a named accredited pharmacy."
- "Monthly. Cancel whenever. We'd rather earn it twelve times than trap you once."

**Limits, stated plainly — this is the signature move:**
- "This will not make you younger. It is not a longevity product and we will not sell it as one."
- "If your panel comes back clean, we will tell you that, and you will not need us."
- "Six of the peptides people ask us for are not legal to compound yet. We will not sell them until they are."

### Never say

Inherited category clichés: *optimize your [anything]* · *unlock your potential* · *best self* · *level up / crush / dominate* · *wellness, redefined* · *the future of [anything]* · *backed by science* · *powered by science* · *doctor-recommended* · *personalized just for you* · *root cause* · *healthspan* · *biohacking* · *transformative* · *game-changing*

Brand-specific bans:
- **"Longevity"** and **"anti-aging."** Age-coded, repels the buyer, and belongs to AgelessRx and Hone.
- **"Optimization."** Fatigued to the point of parody; reads male-gym.
- **"100+ biomarkers."** A dead race we decline to enter.
- **Archetype naming.** No King, Warrior, Alpha, Prime, Elite tiers. That is Maximus and it is embarrassing.
- **Any claim of equivalence to an FDA-approved drug.** Legally dangerous, currently generating warning letters.
- Exclamation points. Emoji. Countdown timers.

## 4. Visual system

Built to be unmistakably *not* Conduit (warm cream, terracotta, Outfit, all-lowercase), *not* Halo (cool steel, Inter), and *not* clinical-blue like the rest of the category.

### Palette

| Role | Color | Hex | Usage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ink | Near-black, cool | `#0E0F13` | Primary substrate, display type on light |
| Ink elevated | Raised dark | `#16181E` | Cards on dark |
| Paper | Cool white | `#FAFAF8` | Light substrate, type on dark |
| Graphite | Mid neutral | `#6B7080` | Secondary type, captions |
| Rule | Hairline | `#E2E3E6` light · `rgba(250,250,248,0.12)` dark | Grid lines, dividers, axes |
| **Signal** | **Indigo-violet** | **`#4B3BFF`** | The bound. CTA, the moving line, the live data point. |
| Signal deep | Pressed state | `#3A2CD6` | Hover/active |
| Positive delta | Restrained green | `#2E7D5B` | Marker improvement only — never decoration |

**Principles:**

- **Indigo-violet is the one color, and it means one thing: the bound.** It marks the line, the current value, and the single action on a screen. If it appears twice on a surface, one of them is wrong.
- **No terracotta, no cream, no warm neutrals.** That is Conduit's language and the two brands must never be mistaken for relatives.
- **No clinical blue.** Owned by Function, Calibrate, and the Hims-adjacent field.
- **No green as a brand color.** AG1 territory. Green appears only as a positive delta on a chart.
- **Light is the default.** Dark surfaces are for data moments — the chart, the panel, the protocol view. Inverted from Conduit, which opens dark.

### Typography

- **Display + body: Instrument Sans** (Google Fonts, free). A precise, slightly narrow grotesque with real character at display size. The name is on-brand by accident, which is a small gift.
- **Data: IBM Plex Mono** (Google Fonts, free). Every number, lab value, dose, date, and eyebrow label sets in mono.

**The mono rule is the signature.** Prose is Instrument Sans; anything measured is Plex Mono. That single discipline makes the brand look like an instrument rather than a supplement company, and no competitor is doing it.

### Wordmark

**`UPPERBOUND` in Instrument Sans 600, uppercase, tracking -0.01em, with a hairline rule set directly above it.**

The rule *is* the mark — it is the literal notation for a bound. Deliberately opposite Conduit's all-lowercase wordmark.

- The rule spans the exact width of the wordmark, 1.5px, in ink or signal indigo.
- **The brand device: the line moves up.** On the homepage it animates upward on load. In the member portal it sits above your tracked marker and rises as the value does. One idea, expressed in the logo, the motion system, and the product.
- Never: title case, italics, outlines, a detached icon, or any color on the wordmark but ink, paper, or signal.

### Layout and motion

- **Grid is visible.** Hairline rules, aligned columns, axis-like structure. The page should feel measured.
- **Data is the hero image.** Charts, panels, and dose schedules are the photography. Where people appear, they are working — desk, airport, gym at 6am — never mid-laugh in a bathrobe.
- **Motion is a single gesture: rise.** Elements enter by moving up. Nothing bounces, spins, or parallaxes.
- **Restraint.** One signal color, one accent per surface, generous whitespace. Premium here is quietness plus precision, not luxury texture.

## 5. Information architecture

```
/                        The capacity argument + the panel + how it works
/protocols               Peptide-native, by outcome — not by molecule
  /protocols/recovery    Tissue repair, training adaptation, sleep quality
  /protocols/output      Cognitive throughput, energy, stress load
  /protocols/composition Body composition, metabolic
  /protocols/vitality    Libido, hormonal, skin
/panel                   What we measure and why. The gate, explained.
/how-it-works            Panel → physician → protocol → retest
/pharmacy                Named partner, accreditations, how compounding works
/evidence/[compound]     One page per compound: what it is, the trial data,
                         what it does not do, current legal status
/pricing                 $249 intake · $99/mo · medication at cost bands
/start                   The intake quiz
/account                 Member portal: protocol, doses, markers, retest, reorder
```

**Two structural choices worth defending:**

Protocols are organized by **outcome**, not molecule — a member arrives knowing they are under-recovered, not knowing they want ipamorelin. But every compound still gets a real `/evidence/` page, because this buyer *will* research the molecule, and being the best source on it is how we win the search they run at 11pm.

Each evidence page carries a **"what this does not do"** section and the compound's **current legal status**. Right now that means stating plainly that BPC-157, TB-500, KPV, MOTS-c, epitalon, and semax were recommended by the PCAC on July 24, 2026 but are not yet legal to compound, and that we will not sell them until the rule is final. Every competitor is either silent on this or already selling them. Saying it out loud is the most credible thing on the entire site.

## 6. The homepage argument

Six beats. Each earns the next.

| Beat | Job | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · Hook | Reframe from decline to capacity | "You're not aging. You're under-recovered." Wordmark rule animates up. |
| 2 · Recognition | Make them feel seen, specifically | The load they actually carry: travel, sleep debt, training that stopped working, output that used to be free. |
| 3 · The gate | Turn friction into proof | "We draw blood before we prescribe anything." The panel, shown as an instrument. |
| 4 · The protocol | Peptide-native, physician-signed | How a protocol is composed, sequenced, and adjusted against the panel. |
| 5 · The delta | The moat, made visible | Six markers, quarterly, charted. The line moving up. |
| 6 · Terms | Confidence as the close | $249 intake · $99/mo · monthly, cancel anytime. No prepay. Named pharmacy, accreditations, "compounded ≠ FDA-approved." |

The close is deliberately the *terms*, not a testimonial. In a category defined by lock-in and vagueness, plain terms are the most persuasive asset available.

## 7. Naming inside the product

No archetypes, no tiers with rank names. Protocols are named for what they address, in the brand's plain register:

**Recovery** · **Output** · **Composition** · **Vitality**

Add-ons are named literally (Sleep, Tissue, Skin). If a name would sound at home on a pre-workout tub, it is wrong.
